INTERACTIVE ARTIFACT VISUAL

Key milestones and professional lessons

Scroll through the sequence to see how technical progress and organizational readiness developed together.

1950Foundations
Turing proposes the imitation game

Introduced a lasting framework for discussing whether machines can demonstrate intelligent behavior.

Lesson: define what success means before testing a system.
1956Birth of AI
Dartmouth formalizes the field

Researchers established artificial intelligence as a distinct area of academic inquiry.

Lesson: shared language helps a new discipline grow.
1960sEarly systems
Language and symbolic reasoning emerge

Programs such as ELIZA and SHRDLU demonstrated both the promise and narrowness of rule-based interaction.

Lesson: impressive demonstrations may still have limited understanding.
1974–1980First AI winter
Expectations exceed capability

Funding declined when systems could not meet ambitious promises under limited computing and data conditions.

Lesson: adoption needs realistic goals and measurable value.
1980sExpert systems
AI enters organizational decision support

Knowledge-based systems applied specialized rules to diagnosis, configuration, and business problems.

Lesson: domain expertise must be captured and maintained.
1987–1993Second AI winter
Maintenance and inflexibility slow growth

Rule-heavy systems became expensive to update, and specialized hardware markets weakened.

Lesson: lifecycle cost and adaptability are part of technical value.
1997Public milestone
Deep Blue defeats the world chess champion

Specialized computing, search, and evaluation demonstrated machine strength in a complex but bounded domain.

Lesson: specialized excellence is not the same as general intelligence.
2006Deep learning revival
Multilayer neural networks regain momentum

Improved training methods, larger datasets, and stronger computing renewed interest in neural networks.

Lesson: old ideas can become practical when infrastructure changes.
2012Breakthrough
Image recognition performance accelerates

Large neural networks trained with graphics processors achieved major gains in visual classification.

Lesson: data and compute can transform model performance.
2016Reinforcement learning
AlphaGo demonstrates strategic learning

Deep networks, search, and reinforcement learning achieved a highly visible milestone in the game of Go.

Lesson: hybrid methods can solve complex decision problems.
2017Transformers
Attention reshapes language AI

The transformer architecture improved how models process relationships across sequences.

Lesson: architecture choices can unlock entirely new product categories.
2020Foundation models
Large language models broaden capability

Pretrained systems became adaptable across writing, summarization, coding, and question answering.

Lesson: general capability increases the need for governance.
2022Mainstream adoption
Conversational generative AI reaches broad users

Accessible interfaces brought large language models into education, business, and everyday work.

Lesson: usability can accelerate adoption faster than policy readiness.
2023–PresentEnterprise AI
Multimodal systems and governance mature together

Organizations evaluate productivity, analytics, and automation while strengthening privacy, oversight, and responsible-use practices.

Lesson: AI value must be balanced with security and accountability.

Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning: A Decision Guide Through Real Cases

Cases compared

Credit-risk prediction with structured data and skin-cancer image classification with complex visual data.

Open full comparison document ↗
What is it?
A concise comparison of traditional machine learning and deep learning using two real-world application domains with different data, interpretation, and resource needs.
Objective
Show why selecting an AI approach should begin with the problem and data rather than assuming that the most complex model is automatically the best choice.
Process
  1. Defined the practical difference between machine learning and deep learning.
  2. Selected one structured-data case and one image-data case.
  3. Compared feature engineering, data volume, computation, and explainability.
  4. Supported the comparison with peer-reviewed research.
  5. Translated technical distinctions into decision criteria for leaders.
