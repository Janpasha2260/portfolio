Workshop OneGenerative AI · Responsible design · Cybersecurity

AI Lab

SafeClick AI: Phishing Awareness and Account Safety Assistant

Audience: students, everyday internet users, and security-aware organizations.

Purpose

Create a calm, privacy-conscious assistant that helps users recognize phishing warning signs, avoid unsafe actions, and contact official support when verification is needed.

Skills Demonstrated

Prompt design, design thinking, GPT Builder, privacy safeguards, scenario testing, responsible AI boundaries, technical documentation, and cybersecurity communication.

Evidence and Process

I defined the user problem, reviewed official guidance, built the prototype, and tested five realistic scenarios. Four tests passed as designed, while one platform safety block was documented as additional evidence instead of being hidden.

Learning Outcomes

I learned that effective AI guidance depends on clear constraints, honest uncertainty, defensive recommendations, privacy protection, and escalation to qualified human support.

Reflection and Self-Assessment

This artifact shows my strongest skills in user-centered safety and responsible AI design. I successfully kept the assistant focused and practical rather than making it a broad cybersecurity chatbot. My next improvement would be a more formal testing rubric with independent reviewers and clearer reporting workflows for schools and workplaces. The work strengthened my ability to combine security expertise with understandable AI guidance.

Workshop TwoModel selection · Research · Explainability

Comparative Analysis

Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning: Choosing the Method That Fits the Problem

Audience: technical professionals and leaders evaluating AI solutions.

Purpose

Explain the practical difference between traditional machine learning and deep learning through credit-risk prediction and skin-cancer image analysis.

Skills Demonstrated

Academic research, comparative analysis, technical writing, model-selection reasoning, data-type evaluation, explainability analysis, and APA-supported evidence.

Evidence and Process

I compared structured financial variables with complex medical-image patterns and evaluated feature engineering, data needs, computational cost, and decision transparency.

Learning Outcomes

I learned that the most complex model is not automatically the best choice. The correct approach depends on the problem, data, interpretability requirements, available resources, and operational risk.

Reflection and Self-Assessment

My strongest result was translating technical differences into a decision that leaders can understand. The two real-world cases made the comparison concrete and supported the claims with peer-reviewed research. A limitation is that the paper does not include a small experimental benchmark. I would strengthen it by adding a visual decision matrix and measurable comparisons of accuracy, training time, and explainability.

Workshop ThreeNeural networks · Python · Adaptive leadership

Python Experiment and Leadership Reflection

Adaptive Neural Network: Learning From a Changed Reality

Audience: AI/ML practitioners, technology leaders, and teams managing organizational change.

Purpose

Demonstrate how a neural network can lose accuracy after a data pattern changes and how retraining with representative information supports adaptation.

Skills Demonstrated

Python, NumPy, scikit-learn, artificial neural networks, ReLU activation, synthetic-data design, concept drift, performance evaluation, visualization, ethical analysis, and change leadership.

Evidence and Process

A small MLPClassifier achieved 94.4% accuracy in the original environment. Accuracy fell to 81.7% after the feature pattern changed and improved to 95.0% after retraining. The complete code and recorded output are included below.

94.4% original81.7% after drift95.0% adapted

Learning Outcomes

I learned how artificial neural networks learn from examples, why activation functions support nonlinear patterns, and why monitoring is necessary when real conditions no longer match training assumptions. The experiment also reinforced the ethical need to label synthetic data and prevent deceptive uses such as deepfakes.

Reflection and Self-Assessment

Neural networks mirror human adaptation because both depend on experience, feedback, and adjustment when familiar patterns stop working. Nehemiah’s response to changing threats—maintaining the mission while changing the method—shows adaptive leadership grounded in faith. My strongest work was connecting measured model behavior to leadership practice. The simulation is intentionally simple; future work should add repeated trials, fairness checks, drift thresholds, stakeholder communication, and human review.
